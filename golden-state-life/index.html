How much coverage do I need?

There isn’t a formula. There is a way people think about it.

Nobody arrives at a number by instinct. Most people work through the same five things, add them up, and then adjust. Here they are.

  • Income replacementHow many years would the people who depend on you need to be covered for, and at roughly what level?
  • Outstanding debtsAnything that would otherwise land on someone else — loans, credit balances, a co-signed obligation.
  • Mortgage balanceUsually the largest single line, and the one most people match a term length to.
  • Childcare and educationWhere it applies, the costs that would still arrive on schedule.
  • Final expensesThe immediate costs a family faces, so that nobody is making financial decisions in a difficult week.

Read that as a starting point, not an answer

The right amount depends on your own circumstances — other coverage you already have, what your household could absorb, and what you actually want to protect. A licensed agent should review it with you before you settle on a figure.

Term life, explained

Written for someone who has never bought insurance.

What term life is

You choose an amount of coverage and a length of time. If you die during that period, the policy pays the people you named. If you don’t, it ends. That is genuinely the whole product.

It has no cash value and it is not an investment — it does not grow, accumulate or return anything. That simplicity is the point of it.

How the term length works

Terms are typically sold in fixed lengths. People generally match the term to the years their household would be exposed — the remaining mortgage, or the years until dependants are independent.

Level premiums

On a level term policy the premium is set at the start and stays the same for the whole term. What that amount is depends on your age, your health, the coverage amount, the term, and the carrier’s underwriting — which is exactly why there is no number anywhere on this website.

What happens at the end

The coverage ends. Depending on the policy there may be options to renew, convert to a permanent policy, or simply let it lapse — each with its own consequences and cost. [CONFIRM which conversion and renewal options their carriers offer]

Permanent coverage — whole life and similar — works differently and costs differently. That is a conversation, not a paragraph.

Who we help

Four situations where this comes up.

None of these are emergencies. They are just the moments when people start thinking about it.

New parents

A household that used to run on two incomes now has someone in it who cannot earn one. That changes the arithmetic.

Homeowners with a mortgage

The most common reason people match a term length to a number of years: the balance owing, and how long it has left to run.

Small business owners
