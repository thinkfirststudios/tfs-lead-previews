What is Connecticut's Business Relationship Disclosure?

Connecticut requires a real estate licensee to explain, early on, whom they represent in a transaction — the buyer, the seller, or both with consent. You will receive it in writing before any substantive discussion about a property. [CONFIRM current required form and wording] Read more.

How do you price a home?

Against town-level comparable sales, read alongside property values, zoning nuances and current demand trends — a disciplined, data-driven approach rather than a number picked to win a listing. [CONFIRM his pricing method in his words]

What does a Connecticut closing involve?

Title work, the buyer's financing conditions, a final walk-through and the signing of closing documents, with attorneys typically involved on each side in Connecticut. [CONFIRM wording with him and a CT attorney]

What does a home inspection cover?

A licensed inspector reviews the structure, roof, mechanical, electrical and plumbing systems and reports on condition. Well, septic, radon and other specialist tests are often arranged separately. [CONFIRM]

How do you get paid?

Compensation is agreed in writing before we work together and is negotiable. [CONFIRM how he wants to describe compensation after recent industry changes]

What should investors know about this market?

General information only, never investment or financial advice: zoning, permitted uses, lot characteristics and the condition of a property shape what is possible. Speak to your own financial and legal advisers. [CONFIRM]

What should I bring to a first conversation?

Your timeline, a rough budget or the address of the home you may sell, any pre-approval letter you already have, and your questions. Nothing else is required.
