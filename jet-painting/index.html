02 / RATIONALE

Why one contractor

A homeowner about to repaint the exterior is usually also looking at a cracked stucco wall, a grey fence and a stained driveway. Four trades, four schedules, four invoices — or one.

Fewer handoffs

Every handoff between trades is a week of waiting and a chance for someone to blame someone else. One crew removes the gaps.

One point of accountability

If the patch telegraphs through the paint, there is exactly one company to call and no argument about whose defect it is.

Sequencing that works

Repairs have cure times. A contractor doing both the repair and the coating schedules around them instead of showing up too early.

The sequence that actually holds up

Drywall→ Texture→ Prime→ Paint
Stucco patch→ Cure→ Masonry primer→ Coat

Skip the cure step and the patch shows through the topcoat within a season. This is the single most common reason an exterior repaint fails early.

03 / DIFFERENTIATOR

Fence & concrete coatings

Nobody else in this market advertises it. It is also the thing sitting in the same field of view as the exterior repaint you were already quoting.

  • PenetratingSoaks into the slab and cures inside the pores. Changes almost nothing about the look, protects against water and salt intrusion, and can't peel because there is no film on top.
  • Film-formingBuilds a visible layer on the surface — the wet-look driveway. Higher gloss, more colour control, but it wears at the traffic lanes and eventually needs recoating.
  • Fence stainPenetrates the grain and lets the wood texture read through. Fades rather than peels, so recoating is a wash-and-reapply.
  • Solid-bodyOpaque, closer to paint, hides weathered or mismatched boards entirely. Longer colour life, but a failure shows as flaking rather than fading.
  • PrepDegrease, etch or grind for concrete; clean and full dry-out for wood. Coatings fail at the prep stage far more often than at the product stage.

05 / REPAIR

Stucco & drywall repair

The repairs that have to happen before any coating goes on — and the reason bundling them with the paint saves a second contractor.

  • Stucco cracksChased open, filled, and feathered well past the repair so the eye can't find the edge.
  • Texture matchDash, lace, sand float or worm — reproduced by hand at the patch. A mismatched patch reads as a scar forever and paint does not hide it.
  • Drywall patchCut back to a stud or backer, taped, and finished to level.
  • Water damageSoft board replaced rather than skimmed over. Find the leak first.
  • BlendingSpot-primed before topcoat so the patch doesn't flash through in a raking light.
