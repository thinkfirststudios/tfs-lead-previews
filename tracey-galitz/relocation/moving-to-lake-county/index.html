The path, step by step

Seven steps from offer letter to keys

  1. 1

    First call & timeline mapping

    We start from the start date and work backwards. When do you need keys? When does a current lease end? Does a home elsewhere need to sell first, and does your employer’s policy set limits on temporary housing? The output is a one-page calendar you can share with HR.

  2. 2

    What you can decide remotely — and what needs you here

    Budget, lender pre-approval, shortlisting towns by commute and housing type, and most document signing can happen from anywhere. Walking a neighbourhood at rush hour, and a final walkthrough of the house you want, are usually worth the trip.

  3. 3

    A scouting trip that doesn't waste a weekend

    Towns are grouped geographically and by commute, homes are pre-screened on video, and the itinerary includes the drive or Metra ride you would actually do on a Monday morning.

  4. 4

    Temporary housing & lease-gap options

    Short-term rentals, furnished apartments and month-to-month leases to bridge the gap between arrival and closing. [CONFIRM what Tracey arranges directly vs refers]

  5. 5

    Offer & inspection while you're out of state

    Live video during the inspection, the full report shared the same day, and a call to talk through repair requests before you respond.

  6. 6

    Closing remotely

    Mail-away closings, power of attorney and e-signature can each remove a flight. [CONFIRM what she and the title companies she uses actually support]

  7. 7

    The first two weeks

    Utilities and municipal services, vehicle registration and driver’s licence updates, property-tax and exemption paperwork, and a short list of local contacts. [CONFIRM checklist items with Tracey]

Commute realities

How you'd actually get to work

Metra's Milwaukee District North line runs out of Libertyville; the Union Pacific North line serves the lakefront towns. By car, I-94 and the Tri-State carry most trips toward Chicago, O'Hare, Kenosha and Milwaukee.

Commute times change with schedules, construction and the hour you travel. Every figure on this page will carry a cited source and a date — because stale commute numbers are worse than none.
