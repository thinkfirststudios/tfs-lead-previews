Buying a Coral Springs Condo

What to check before you fall for the unit.

The things that trip condo buyers up are rarely inside the unit. They're in the association.

  • Association approval & interviews

    Many condo associations review buyers before closing — often an application, and in some buildings an interview. What's required and how long it takes depends on the building. [CONFIRM per building]

  • HOA dues & what they cover

    Dues may or may not include items such as building insurance, grounds, amenities or some utilities. The association's budget shows exactly what yours pay for.

  • Reserves & special assessments

    An association's reserves, and any assessment it is planning, affect what you pay after closing. Florida's condo reserve and inspection rules have changed in recent years. [CONFIRM with counsel / current Florida statute]

  • Florida condo financing

    Lenders review the building as well as the buyer, and some buildings are not “warrantable” for certain loan types. It pays to check this before you write an offer.

  • Pet & lease restrictions

    Rules on pets, renting and minimum lease terms are set by each association's governing documents. [CONFIRM per building]

  • Age-restricted (55+) communities

    Some Florida communities lawfully operate as housing for older persons. Whether a building does is a factual question answered by its documents. [CONFIRM per building]

  • What an estoppel letter is

    An estoppel certificate from the association states what is owed on a unit, so unpaid dues or assessments don't carry over to the buyer at closing.

Frequently Asked

General information about condos in Florida, not legal or financial advice.

How does association approval work?

Most condo associations have their own approval process for buyers — often an application, and in some buildings an interview. What's required, what it costs and how long it takes depends on the building's documents. [CONFIRM per building]

What do HOA dues cover?

It varies by association. Dues may cover items such as building insurance, grounds, amenities or some utilities. The association's budget shows exactly what they pay for. [CONFIRM per building]

Can I rent my condo out?

Leasing rules are set by each association and can include tenant approval and minimum lease terms. [CONFIRM per building]

How is condo financing different?

Lenders look at the building as well as the buyer — its budget, reserves and insurance among other things. Some buildings don't qualify for certain loan programmes, so it's worth checking early. General information, not financial advice.
