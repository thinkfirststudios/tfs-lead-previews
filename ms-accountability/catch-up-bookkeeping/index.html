The process

What a cleanup actually looks like

Four stages. You will know which one you are in at any point.

1

Scope it

How many months, how many accounts, what state the records are in. This is where the size of the job stops being frightening and becomes a number.

2

Gather

Bank and card statements for the period, and access to whatever software exists. [CONFIRM the secure intake process — no bank credentials are ever collected through the website]

3

Reconstruct and reconcile

Transactions categorised month by month and matched against the statements, so the finished books agree with what actually happened.

4

Close and hand over

Each period closed out, statements produced, and everything in a state your tax preparer can work from.

In plain English

A cleanup is not “doing your books again”. It is going back through the bank record and rebuilding what happened, in order, until the software and the bank tell the same story.

What’s needed from you

Less than you think.

The most common reason a cleanup stalls is someone trying to tidy up before handing it over. Don’t — hand it over as it is.

  • Bank and card statementsFor the whole period, including the accounts you forgot about.
  • Access to the softwareIf there is any. If there isn’t, that is fine too. [CONFIRM software]
  • Receipts you haveWhatever exists. Missing ones are a normal part of a cleanup, not a blocker.
  • Context on the odd onesThe handful of transactions only you can explain. Usually a short call rather than homework.
What isn’t needed

You do not need to sort anything into categories, reconcile anything yourself, or apologise for the state of it. That is the job you are hiring out.
