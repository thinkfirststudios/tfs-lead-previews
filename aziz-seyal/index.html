Investor / REO track

Bank-owned inventory, handled with care.

An REO purchase is not a conventional purchase with a lower price. The seller is a lender, the property is usually sold as-is, the addenda are the bank's, and the timelines run on its schedule. The investor track explains how these transactions actually work before you write an offer.

What does the Business Relationship Disclosure mean?

Connecticut requires a real estate licensee to disclose, in writing, whom they represent in a transaction — so you know from the start whether the agent is working for you, for the other side, or in another capacity. You will receive it before any substantive discussion about a specific property. Read more. [CONFIRM current required form and wording]

How does an REO purchase differ from a conventional one?

A bank-owned (REO) property is sold by a lender, usually as-is, with the lender's own addenda and response timelines. Inspections are for your information rather than negotiation leverage in the usual sense, and some financing types may be constrained by the property's condition. How REO purchases work. This is general information, not financial or legal advice.

What does a short sale involve?

A short sale is a sale where the seller's lender agrees to accept less than the amount owed. The lender must approve the terms, which adds a separate approval step and can extend the timeline. General information only. [CONFIRM wording]
