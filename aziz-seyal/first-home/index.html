First-time buyer pathway

Your first home, in plain order.

Seven steps — what each is, what it is not, and what you sign. Available in each of the five languages [CONFIRM].

  1. Pre-approval

    A lender reviews your income, assets and credit and states how much it is prepared to lend, subject to conditions. It is not a loan commitment, and it does not lock a rate. It tells sellers — and you — that financing has been looked at.

  2. Down payment

    The down payment is your share of the price; closing costs are separate. How much you need depends on the loan program. [CONFIRM no figures published without a sourced, dated reference]

  3. Business Relationship Disclosure

    Before any substantive discussion about a specific property, Connecticut requires a written disclosure of whom the agent represents. You read it, ask questions and sign to acknowledge receipt. What it is. [CONFIRM current required form and wording]

  4. Viewing

    Homes are seen with a checklist: roof, heating, water, electrical, signs of moisture, and the running costs that come with the property.

  5. Offer

    Price, deposit, contingencies (financing, inspection, appraisal) and dates are written up, explained line by line, then submitted.

  6. Inspection

    A licensed home inspector reports on condition. The report is read together, and you decide what, if anything, to raise with the seller.

  7. Closing

    Final walkthrough, a review of the closing documents, signatures and keys.

General information about the buying process in Connecticut — not legal, tax or lending advice.
