The four things

What comes off

Connecticut summers are humid, and a north-facing wall in shade barely dries out between rains. That is exactly the environment algae and mildew need, which is why the shaded side of a house always goes first.

Dirt

Road film, pollen and airborne grime that builds up as a flat grey haze across the whole elevation.

Mould

Dark patches in the shaded, damp parts of a wall. Surface growth on the exterior only — this is cleaning, not remediation.

Mildew

The speckled black dotting that shows up first under eaves and behind shrubs, where air never moves.

Algae

The green film, and the black streaking on roofs. It has a root structure — pressure alone knocks the top off and it grows straight back.

The technical difference

Soft wash vs. high pressure

This is a genuine advantage over the pressure-washing guys, and right now nobody knows they have it.

Soft Wash Their system

  • Low pressure plus a cleaning solution — the solution does the work, not the force
  • Kills organic growth at the root, so it stays clean longer instead of regrowing
  • Won't force water behind siding or into wall cavities
  • Won't strip paint, raise wood grain, or dislodge roof granules
  • Safe for shingles, where high pressure can void a roofing warranty

High Pressure The blunt tool

  • Force alone — blasts the surface layer off and leaves the roots behind
  • Growth returns quickly because nothing was actually killed
  • Can drive water behind siding and trim
  • Can etch wood, strip paint and gouge soft masonry
  • Right tool for hard flat surfaces — wrong tool for a wall

Connecticut, specifically

It comes back. That's the business.

Exterior cleaning is not one-and-done in this climate, and framing it as recurring maintenance is worth more than framing it as a one-off job.

Spring

Pollen coats everything for weeks. The first wash of the year is usually the one people actually notice.

Summer

Humidity plus shade equals algae and mildew. North elevations and anything behind shrubs go first.

Fall

Wet leaf litter stains decks, walkways and the base of siding where it piles up against the wall.

Winter into spring

Road salt and winter grime carried onto walkways, steps and the lower parts of the elevation.
