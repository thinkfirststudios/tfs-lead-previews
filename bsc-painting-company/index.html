The part painters skip

Texture matching

This is what homeowners are actually afraid of. A stucco patch that doesn't match reads as a scar on the wall forever — you see it every time you pull into the driveway, and paint does not hide it.

Matching means reading the original texture — dash, lace, sand float, worm — and reproducing it by hand at the patch, feathered out far enough past the repair that the eye can't find the edge. It is the difference between a wall that was fixed and a wall that looks like it was never broken.

  • Crack chasing, patching and blending
  • Texture matched by hand to the existing finish
  • Elastomeric coatings over hairline cracking
  • Re-dash where the original finish is beyond patching

Structural stucco and lath work typically fall under a C-35 Lathing & Plastering classification rather than C-33. [CONFIRM C-35 or scope limitation] — nothing on this page advertises stucco beyond a confirmed licensed scope.

Cabinets, sprayed properly

Brushed cabinet doors always look brushed. Doors come off, get labelled, degreased and sanded, then sprayed on racks so the finish lays down flat. Boxes and face frames are finished in place.

  • Doors removed, numbered and racked
  • Degreased — kitchen cabinets carry years of cooking oil
  • Bonding primer chosen for the substrate
