The selling path

The price is set by the market. The preparation is set by you.

Two of these five stages are genuinely in your control. Knowing which two is most of the job.

Stage 01

Preparation

A walkthrough, and an honest list of what is worth doing versus what will not return its cost. Paint, decluttering, landscaping and small repairs usually earn back. Kitchens, generally, do not — not when you are selling next month.

Worth knowing: the list should get shorter as the conversation goes on, not longer. An agent adding work is not automatically an agent adding value.
Stage 02

Pricing — explained, not promised

Price comes from what comparable properties nearby actually sold for, adjusted for the differences between them and yours. That is a comparative market analysis. It is not an appraisal, and it is not the automated estimate a portal shows you.

Any agent can promise a number to win the listing. The market then declines to honour it, and the property sits — which costs more than pricing it correctly on day one ever would have.

Worth knowing: a listing that has sat gets read as a listing with something wrong with it, whatever the actual reason.
Stage 03

Marketing plan

Professional photography, the MLS entry filled in properly so it surfaces in the right searches, syndication to the portals buyers actually use, and social distribution. [CONFIRM what is included — photography, video, staging, print]

Worth knowing: the first photo does most of the work. Everything else in the plan is amplification of that one decision.
Stage 04

Negotiation

Offers arrive as packages, not prices. Terms, deposit, contingency periods, close date and financing type all change what an offer is worth to you — sometimes more than the headline number does.

Worth knowing: the highest offer and the best offer are frequently not the same offer, and the difference usually shows up in whether it closes.
Stage 05

Closing

Disclosures, inspection response, appraisal, loan funding and recording. As a seller, disclosure is the part with the longest tail — California requires specific written disclosures, and getting them complete protects you after the sale, not just during it.
