Home/Seniors & Downsizing

SRES · Seniors Real Estate Specialist

Selling the house you raised a family in.

A guide for homeowners who are right-sizing, and for the adult children helping them — sequenced the way the decision actually unfolds. Take it at your own pace.

Start with step one ↓

General information about real estate — not legal, tax, financial or medical advice. Probate and estate matters require an attorney; tax questions belong with a CPA. 01 — Right-sizing

What fits the next fifteen years, not the last forty

The house worked for the life that happened in it. The question now is what the next home needs to do: fewer stairs or none, less to maintain, closer to the people and places that matter day to day. Starting from the next chapter, rather than from what is being given up, usually makes every later decision easier.

What people get wrong here

Choosing the next home by square footage alone. The better question is which rooms get used every week — and which exist mostly to hold things.

Market timing and life timing are two different clocks.
02 — Timing a move

When to sell is two questions, not one

Market timing is about season, inventory and pricing. Life timing is about health, family, a lease or a community’s move-in date, and readiness. They rarely line up perfectly, and when they conflict, life timing usually should win. A plan that works in either season is worth more than a guess about the market.

What people get wrong here

Waiting for the “perfect” market while the life reasons for moving quietly become more urgent.

Who is in the room, and who decides?
03 — Working with adult children

Three siblings, three states, one decision

Families do this differently. Sometimes the homeowner decides and the children help; sometimes a power of attorney is involved; often there are several opinions arriving by phone. Agreeing early on who makes the final call, who needs to be kept informed, and how updates will be shared keeps the process calm — and keeps the homeowner at the centre of it.

What people get wrong here

Talking about the parent instead of with them. Whoever owns the home should be part of every conversation they are able to be part of.

04 — Probate & estate sales

An executor’s timeline is not a seller’s timeline

When a home is sold as part of an estate, the executor or administrator usually needs legal authority before a sale can close, and an estate attorney guides that process. There may also be tax questions for a CPA. The real estate side — preparing, pricing and marketing the property — runs alongside, on a schedule the legal process sets.

What people get wrong here

Listing before confirming who has the authority to sign. Talk to an estate attorney first. [CONFIRM the full scope of what she does and does not handle on estate sales]

05 — The logistics nobody warns you about

Decades of contents, one closing date

Sorting what stays, what goes to family, what is sold and what is donated takes longer than anyone expects. Movers who specialise in downsizing, estate-sale companies and clean-out services can help. Then the closing needs to line up with a move-in date — at a new home or a community — so nobody is caught between the two.

What people get wrong here

Setting a closing date before the contents plan exists. Sort first, then schedule.
