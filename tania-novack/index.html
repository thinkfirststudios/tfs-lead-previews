Designations

CRS, SFR, SRES: what those letters actually mean

Most buyers have never been told. Here is each one in plain English — and what it changes about how Tania works.

CRS

Certified Residential Specialist

What it is
An advanced designation for agents who focus on residential sales, earned through additional coursework and documented experience.
What it changes
Residential buying and selling is the core of the work, not a sideline.
Received
2019
Awarded by
Residential Real Estate Council

Her profile cites “only 29,000 REALTORS nationwide” holding CRS — [CONFIRM source + year: Residential Real Estate Council]

SFR

Short Sales & Foreclosure Resource

What it is
Training in how distressed sales work — short sales, bank-owned property and foreclosure-related transactions.
What it changes
When a sale is distressed, the timeline and the paperwork differ, and lender approval sits in the middle of it. She knows what to expect.
Awarded by
National Association of REALTORS® [CONFIRM]

General information, not legal or debt advice. A real estate agent cannot stop a foreclosure. [CONFIRM exactly what she does in a short sale]

SRES

Seniors Real Estate Specialist

What it is
A designation focused on the real estate decisions people make later in life — selling a long-held home, right-sizing, relocating closer to family.
What it changes
The conversation starts with what the next home needs to do, and includes the family members who are part of the decision.
Awarded by
SRES® Council, National Association of REALTORS® [CONFIRM]

What does a buyer’s agent actually do?

A buyer’s agent helps you find and evaluate homes, understand comparable sales, prepare and negotiate an offer, and coordinate inspections, the mortgage process and closing with your attorney.

How is Tania paid?

[CONFIRM] Compensation is agreed in writing before you work together. Details to be confirmed with Tania and REMAX House Values.

What does SRES mean for my parents?

SRES (Seniors Real Estate Specialist) is a designation focused on the real estate decisions people make later in life — right-sizing, timing a move and working with family. It is not legal, tax or financial advice; those questions go to an attorney or CPA. The seniors guide →
