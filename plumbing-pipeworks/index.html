01 / METHOD

Measure. Locate. Verify.

Three stages, in that order. The order is the point — every stage narrows the search area, so that by the time anything is opened, we already know what is behind it.

Stage

01

Measure

Isolate the system and put a pressure test on it. Watch the meter with every fixture off. This answers a binary question: is there a leak at all, and is it on the supply side or somewhere else?

Nothing has been opened yet.

Stage

02

Locate

Narrow it down without breaking anything. Acoustic equipment listens for the leak through the slab. Thermal imaging reads the temperature difference a hot-line leak leaves on the floor. A camera goes down the line where a line is accessible.

Still nothing opened.

Stage

03

Verify

Cross-check the location with a second method before a single tool touches the concrete. Two independent readings agreeing on the same spot is what turns a probable area into a pinpoint.

Now — and only now — we open one small area.

The whole argument: we pinpoint the leak before opening your slab, wall or driveway — so the repair is one small access point instead of an exploratory demolition.

INST / 01

Thermal imaging

Reads surface temperature. A hot-water line leaking under a slab warms the floor above it in a shape you can see.

  • FindsHot-line slab leaks, wet insulation, moisture behind finishes
  • Saves youGuesswork demolition across a whole floor
  • Owned[CONFIRM]
INST / 02

Pressure test & meter check

The cheapest, most decisive first step. Isolate, pressurise, and watch. Either it holds or it doesn't.

  • FindsWhether a leak exists, and which system it is on
  • Saves youPaying to investigate a leak that isn't there
  • Owned[CONFIRM]
INST / 03

Acoustic detection

Listens for the sound a pressurised leak makes escaping through concrete, soil or wall cavity.

  • FindsPressurised supply leaks under slab and in walls
  • Saves youOpening the wrong wall
  • Owned[CONFIRM]
INST / 04

Line camera

Goes down an accessible line and shows the condition rather than inferring it. Drain and sewer side rather than pressurised supply.

  • FindsBreaks, offsets, root intrusion, bellies
  • Saves youReplacing a line that only needed a spot repair
  • Owned[CONFIRM]
INST / 05

Line tracing

Establishes where the pipe actually runs before anyone digs. The route on the plans and the route in the ground are frequently different.

  • FindsBuried route and depth of supply and irrigation lines
  • Saves youA trench in the wrong place
  • Owned[CONFIRM]
03 / HIGHEST VALUE

Slab leaks

The job people are most afraid of, because the pipe is under the floor they are standing on.

  • SymptomsA warm patch on the floor, the sound of water with everything off, a bill that climbed for no reason, unexplained damp at the base of a wall.
  • DetectionPressure test to confirm, then thermal and acoustic to locate, then a second method to verify before anything is opened.
  • Spot repairOpen one small access point and repair the failure. Least disruptive, when the rest of the line is sound. [CONFIRM they perform this]
  • RerouteAbandon the under-slab section and run a new line overhead or through walls, leaving the floor alone. [CONFIRM]
  • RepipeReplace the affected system where the pipe has failed in more than one place. [CONFIRM]

05 / IRRIGATION

Irrigation lines

The second stated specialty, and the one most general plumbers hand back. An irrigation leak runs continuously into soil where nobody sees it — it shows up on the bill long before it shows up on the lawn.

  • Line leaksUnderground lateral and mainline breaks, often at a fitting or where a root has moved the pipe.
  • ValvesA valve that fails to close keeps a whole zone running quietly underground.
  • ManifoldsValve box assemblies where multiple connections give multiple failure points.
  • Dead zonesA patch of lawn that never greens up is usually a broken lateral, not a watering schedule.
