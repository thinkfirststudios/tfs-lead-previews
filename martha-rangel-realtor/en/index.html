Mobile & manufactured homes

Buying a mobile home is not like buying a house

Comprar una casa móvil no es como comprar una casa

Seven differences that decide the whole purchase. Read them before you sign anything — including before you sign with any agent.

1

Space rent

La renta del espacio

In a park you own the home but you rent the land it sits on. That monthly space rent is a permanent cost, completely separate from the purchase price, and it can go up.

It is the number buyers find out about too late, so it appears on every listing card on this site as its own line. [CONFIRM typical space rent ranges in her parks — publish no number until she confirms it]

2

Title: HCD or real property

El título: HCD o propiedad real

Most manufactured homes inside a park are titled through the California Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) as personal property — closer to a vehicle registration than to a deed.

A home on land you own can be attached to a permanent foundation and converted to real property. This one distinction drives almost everything else on this page.

3

Financing works differently

El financiamiento funciona diferente

Because an HCD-titled home usually is not real property, a conventional mortgage often does not apply. Buyers generally look at chattel (personal property) loans, or FHA Title I / Title II depending on the title status.

No rate, term or approval is promised anywhere on this site. The point is to know which path applies to you before you fall in love with a home. [CONFIRM preferred lender referrals, if any]

4

Park approval

La aprobación del parque

The park management usually runs its own application and residency approval, on top of the purchase itself. A buyer of a traditional house never encounters this step — and it has its own timing and its own paperwork.

5

Age-restricted communities

Comunidades con restricción de edad

Many parks are 55+ communities; others are all-age. That is a lawful housing-for-older-persons designation and it changes who is eligible to buy there. [CONFIRM which communities she works in and the restriction for each]

6

Transfer, escrow and taxes

Traspaso, escrow e impuestos

Transferring an HCD-titled home goes through HCD rather than a county deed recording, and the cost structure is different from a traditional closing. [CONFIRM specifics with her escrow partner]

7

What to inspect

Qué revisar en la inspección

Manufactured homes have their own checklist: the foundation and tie-downs, the roof, the skirting, the HVAC, and whether any addition — a porch, a carport, a room — was permitted.

Selling your home

Two different sales, two different processes

Dos ventas distintas, dos procesos distintos

Selling a mobile home

Vender una casa móvil

  1. Notify the park and check what its rules require of a seller.
  2. Confirm the title status with HCD before listing, not after an offer.
  3. Price with the space rent in view — it affects what a buyer can carry.
  4. Plan around the buyer’s park approval, which has its own timeline.
  5. Transfer through HCD at closing. [CONFIRM escrow partner and fee structure]

Selling a traditional home

Vender una casa tradicional

  1. Walk the property and agree what is worth doing before photos.
  2. Price from comparable sales, not from a website estimate.
  3. Photograph, list on the MLS and market it in both languages.
  4. Review offers and terms together, in the language you prefer.
  5. Escrow, inspections, appraisal, close. [CONFIRM typical timeline]

Glossary

The words that keep coming up

Las palabras que siempre salen

Short, plain definitions in both languages. Nobody else in this market publishes one.

Escrow

Escrow (depósito en garantía)

A neutral third party holds the money and the documents until every condition of the sale is met, then completes the transfer.

HCD title

Título de HCD

Registration of a manufactured home through the California Department of Housing and Community Development, as personal property rather than deeded real estate.

Space rent

Renta del espacio

The monthly amount paid to the park for the land your home sits on. Separate from the purchase price, ongoing, and subject to change.

Chattel loan

Préstamo chattel

A loan secured by personal property rather than real estate — the common route for an HCD-titled home. Terms differ from a mortgage.

Contingency

Contingencia

A condition written into the contract that must be satisfied — an inspection, an appraisal, a loan — or the deal can be cancelled under its terms.

Park approval

Aprobación del parque

The park’s own residency application, separate from the purchase, which a buyer must pass before moving in.

55+ community

Comunidad de 55+

A community lawfully designated as housing for older persons, where age requirements apply to residents.

Tie-downs

Anclajes (tie-downs)

The anchoring system that secures a manufactured home to its foundation. One of the first things to check in an inspection.
