Frequently Asked Questions

General information about New Jersey residential transactions — not legal advice.

How does attorney review work in New Jersey?

Residential sale contracts in New Jersey that are prepared by licensees generally include an attorney-review period after signing, during which either party's attorney can review the contract and, within that window, cancel it. The length and conditions of that period are set out in the contract itself. [CONFIRM period + wording]

What does a buyer's agent actually do?

A buyer's agent helps define the search, arranges viewings, gathers information on comparable sales, prepares and negotiates the offer, and coordinates the steps between contract and closing with the lender, inspector and attorneys.

Who pays the agent?

Compensation is agreed in writing and can vary from one transaction to the next. It is discussed and documented before you work together. [CONFIRM]

How long does a New Jersey sale take?

It depends on financing, inspection findings, attorney review, title work and the parties' own schedules. Your agent, lender and attorney can give a realistic timeline for your situation. [CONFIRM]

What should I bring to a first conversation?

A rough budget, the areas you are considering, must-haves versus nice-to-haves, your timing, and — if you are buying with a mortgage — any pre-approval you already have.

What happens at inspection?

A licensed home inspector examines the property's visible systems and structure and produces a written report. Buyers then decide, with advice, whether to proceed, request repairs or credits, or rely on any contract contingency.

What is a pre-approval, and what is it not?

A pre-approval is a lender's conditional review of your income, assets and credit indicating what it may lend. It is not a final loan commitment; the property, appraisal and final underwriting still have to be approved.
