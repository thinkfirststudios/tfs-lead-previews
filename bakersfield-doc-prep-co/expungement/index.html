What a petition for dismissal actually does

In California, what people usually mean by “expungement” is a petition asking the court to dismiss a conviction after the sentence and probation are complete. If the court grants it, the record is updated to show the case was dismissed.

And what it does not do

This is the part that matters most, because people make real decisions based on getting it wrong.

  • It is not an erasureThe record does not disappear. It shows the case as dismissed. It remains a record.
  • It does not hide the case from everyoneCertain agencies and certain kinds of application can still see it. [CONFIRM the accurate general description here — this needs to be right, and it needs a source]
  • It does not restore every rightSome consequences of a conviction are not undone by a dismissal. [CONFIRM]
  • It is not automaticThe court decides, and it can decline.

If any of the above affects a decision you are making — about a job application, a licence, immigration, or anything else — talk to an attorney. That is not a disclaimer we are adding to be safe. The consequences of getting this wrong land on you, not on us.
