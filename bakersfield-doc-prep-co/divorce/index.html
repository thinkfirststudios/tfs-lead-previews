About the court’s filing fee

The court charges its own fee to file a divorce, and it is separate from our $500. If paying it would be a hardship, the court has a fee waiver process — ask the clerk or check the court’s self-help resources. [CONFIRM the current fee and the Kern County Superior Court self-help URL]

Uncontested and contested are different situations

Uncontested means you and your spouse agree on the terms — or the other side does not respond. The paperwork is the main task, and that is squarely what document preparation is for.

Contested means you do not agree, and something has to be decided by the court. That usually needs an attorney, because what it needs is advice and advocacy rather than typing. [CONFIRM whether he assists with contested matters at all, and in what capacity]

How long does it take?

That is set by California law and by the court’s own calendar, not by us. There is a statutory waiting period, and the court moves at the pace it moves at.

When to talk to an attorney instead

  • Custody is in dispute
  • Property division is complicated — a business, a pension, multiple properties
  • There is domestic violence involved
  • You need someone to advise you on what to ask for
